# Trust Microcopy

Task 8, Step 3. Five strings, reused across every product page. Written once here, approved once,
then used verbatim by whichever stack wins. Inconsistent wording across pages reads as carelessness.

---

| Slot | String | Status |
|------|--------|--------|
| Return line | "7-day returns on damaged or incorrect items. Read our policy." | ☐ approved |
| Colour disclaimer | "Colours may vary slightly across screens." | ☐ approved |
| Unique badge | "One-of-a-kind piece — only one available." | ☐ approved |
| WhatsApp CTA | "Questions? Message us on WhatsApp." | ☐ approved |
| Payment assurance | "Secure payments via `<<FILL: gateway name | after Task 9>>`." | ☐ blocked on Task 9 |

## Rules for use

- **The unique badge appears only where `is_unique = TRUE`.** Showing it on a restockable design is
  a false scarcity claim, prohibited by PRD §8.4 — along with fabricated reviews and fake MRPs.
- **The return line links to the actual policy.** A trust claim that does not link to the thing it
  claims is decoration.
- **The payment assurance names the real gateway.** "Secure payments" alone signals nothing; a name
  a buyer recognises does the work.
- The WhatsApp CTA must open a real WhatsApp thread to the business number, pre-filled with the
  product name where the platform allows it. The buyer should not have to explain which saree they
  are asking about.

## What is deliberately absent

No countdown timers. No "12 people are viewing this". No invented "only 2 left" on restockable
stock. No review stars before real reviews exist.

Every one of those raises short-term conversion and costs the thing this business actually runs on.
The whole customer base arrives by referral from someone who vouched for the shop — a buyer who
feels manipulated does not just fail to return, they stop referring.
